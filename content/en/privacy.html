4. Location and distance

  • The app asks for an approximate location, only while you are using it. It does not follow your movements in the background.
  • The server rounds the coordinates it receives to a grid of about 500 m before saving them; the original coordinates are neither kept nor logged. Only the latest square is kept. A square measures about 500 m from north to south; from east to west, its width depends on latitude: at least about 480 m, and up to about 1 km north of 52° latitude.
  • Other people never receive your coordinates, only a band: “less than 1 km” or “about N km”. In a conversation, this band is recalculated at most once a day (the day is counted in Brussels time, whatever your time zone). Beyond 100 km, the band is coarser: rounded to the nearest 10 km up to 1000 km, then to the nearest 100 km. The exact distance is never shown.
  • Limit the distance is optional (from 1 to 100 km, in the filters). This limit only affects what you see: it does not restrict who can see you. While the publisher keeps the “no limit” option open, an account that has never saved filters has no limit (filters already saved keep theirs), and compatible people who do not limit the distance can see your profile even if they are far away. The publisher may restore a maximum limit for everyone later: “no limit” then means 100 km, and distances beyond that are no longer shown.
  • With the “Hide my distance” option, no band is shown about you, either in the sky or on your profile, and no distance appears in your conversations, for either person. In the sky and the grid, your profile is not ranked by distance: it takes a randomly chosen spot (10 profiles with a hidden distance at most per sky, drawn again every day). Who can see you depends on the other person's filter. If they limit the distance, only when you are less than 25 km from them, whatever limit they chose: seeing you then tells them you are within that radius. If they do not limit it, you are drawn at random from all compatible profiles, with no radius: seeing you then tells them nothing about your distance. You still see the approximate distance of others in the sky and on profiles.
  • Invisible mode removes your profile from other people's sky and grid. Your conversations remain, and no distance appears in them, for either person. It also stops profile visits from being recorded. With build 3, you also always appear as “Offline” (see the “Online” status).
  • Who can still open your profile. In invisible mode, or when your location has not been updated for more than 7 days, you appear neither in the sky nor in the grid. Your profile page and profile photos can then be viewed only by a person you have a conversation with, or a person you sent a signal that is still pending (neither accepted nor passed) and has not expired, with no indication of distance. A signal you send in invisible mode therefore opens your profile to the person who receives it. A signal you only received from someone does not give them this access. A block, in either direction, closes it.
  • Even an approximate distance can help a determined person guess your area. Hiding your distance removes this clue, but does not remove you from other people's sky: with a distance limit turned on, a person who sees you there knows you are less than 25 km away, and by changing their location several times they may try to narrow down where you are. Limiting your own distance does not protect your location. Invisible mode removes you from the sky and the grid, but does not close your profile to the people described above; block those you no longer want to show it to. None of these settings erases what others have already seen or noted, or guarantees that nobody can guess your area: your photos and messages can give clues too.
  • If you withdraw the “location” consent, the stored location is erased immediately and your profile is disabled.

6. What others see

  • Compatible people see your first name, your age, your approved photos, your answers to questions, your current moods and, unless you hide it, an approximate distance; they may be far away (see the distance limit). “Something casual” plans are shown only to people who turned them on too. With build 3, people who can see your profile also see your “Online” status, unless you hide it. The grid view of the sky shows your photo, first name and age. In invisible mode or with a location that is too old, access is more restricted and shows no distance (see who can still open your profile).
  • If you have turned on “Show my orientation”, people who can open your profile page see your orientation there (up-to-date version of the app). Otherwise it does not appear.
  • They see neither your email address, nor your date of birth, nor your location.
  • A person you write to receives your signals, messages and meeting suggestions. They can copy or screenshot them: do not send anything you would not want to see shared.
  • A blocked person no longer sees you and can no longer write to you. They are not told.

Profile visits: “Viewed your profile” and “Visit discreetly”

  • What counts as a visit: opening a person's profile, for example from the sky, the grid, a conversation or the list of your visitors. The two accounts and the date of the latest visit, to the nearest hour, are recorded; a new visit replaces the previous one.
  • What the visited person sees, in “Viewed your profile” (in My profile; from build 3 of the app{{if not (on "build3_distribue")}}, not yet distributed{{end}}, in the Signals tab, under “Visits”): your first name, age, photo and the day of your visit, with no distance; they can open your profile. They receive no notification. The list shows 100 people at most, and only those who are still active, compatible, visible, with a recent location, and with no block between you.
  • Badge for new visits (build 3): for it, the server keeps with each visit a “viewed” or not indicator, with no date of viewing, erased along with the visit. The badge only counts the visits the list would show, and opening the tab marks them as viewed. Visits recorded before this indicator was introduced are treated as viewed. No notification is sent for all that.
  • “Visit discreetly” (in My profile, off by default): your visits are not recorded, neither are the visits others pay you during that time, and you do not see who viewed your profile. Invisible mode has the same effect.
  • Visits already recorded are not erased by these settings. With discreet visiting, the visits you made earlier remain visible to the people concerned; invisible mode hides them while it is on. The visits you had received reappear when you leave these modes, if their retention period has not passed.
  • Retention: 30 days after the latest visit, or until one of the two accounts is erased. A block or a disabled profile does not erase them: they are no longer shown.
  • The list and the setting are in the up-to-date version of the app. With an older version, your visits are recorded in the same way; invisible mode prevents it.

“Online” status

This feature comes from build 3 of the app{{if not (on "build3_distribue")}}, not yet distributed{{end}}.

  • What is shown: “Online” if you were active in the last 15 minutes, otherwise “Offline”. It is a yes or a no: no time is sent to others. Your activity is recorded at most once every 2 minutes: the status may therefore switch off 13 to 15 minutes after the latest one.
  • Who sees it: people who can see your profile, with build 3 and without hiding their own status, in the sky and the grid, on your profile, in a conversation and in their favourites. A blocked person sees nothing of it.
  • What counts as activity: the app's exchanges with the server and its live connection while it is on screen.
  • On by default and reciprocal: with “Hide my status” (in My profile), you appear as “Offline”, you no longer see other people's status and you can no longer use the “Online” filter. Nothing more is recorded for this status and the latest activity kept for it is erased immediately.
  • Invisible mode: you always appear as “Offline”. Your activity is still recorded for this status, and this mode does not stop you from seeing other people's.
  • Older version: with a version earlier than build 3, no activity is recorded for this status and you appear as “Offline”.
  • Retention: only the latest activity is kept, with no history; it is erased when you hide your status, otherwise with the account. The status triggers no notification and is not written to technical logs.
  • Limitation: even with no time shown, a person who often looks at your profile or their sky may notice when you are online. To prevent this, hide your status.

“Something casual” plans

One more kind of plan, offered from build 3 of the app{{if not (on "build3_distribue")}}, not yet distributed{{end}}. Like the others, it is optional, counts towards the maximum of 3 plans and is set for a given time.

Private photos and albums

  • Who sees them: nobody, as long as you do not send them or share an album. Sending or sharing is only possible in a conversation open to both of you (accepted signal, active accounts, no block), to a person who has not turned on “Refuse photos”.
  • No prior check: nobody checks a private photo before it is sent or shared, and no automatic analysis of the image is performed. Sexually explicit content is forbidden there as everywhere else: the rule is enforced following a report (removal, sanctions).
  • Receiving: the photo appears blurred. This blur is a pattern created on the phone: nothing from the image is transmitted before the person touches it to display it.
  • Sending modes: normal (until you withdraw it), view once (10 seconds) or disappearing 24 hours after sending. A shared album follows your changes: an added photo becomes visible, a removed photo disappears.
  • Withdrawing: at any time you can withdraw a sent photo, delete a photo or an album, or remove access to an album. What has already been seen cannot be taken back. A block permanently closes sending and sharing between you; unblocking reopens nothing.

  • Screenshots: they are blocked on Android only, not on iPhone, and another device can always photograph the screen. Only send what you would accept seeing passed around.
  • Files and limits: images are re-encoded without metadata and stored separately. A photo sent from your phone's gallery does not go into your private gallery, but it is stored on the server. Sending and sharing (30 per 24 hours) and the space per account are limited.
  • After they disappear: a copy of a photo shown at least once is kept for a while (see retention periods).

11. Security

  • Encrypted connections (HTTPS) between your device and the service, through Cloudflare.
  • Passwords and tokens kept as hashes.
  • Location rounded before saving; photo metadata removed; photo addresses signed and temporary.
    • {{- if on "photos_privees"}}
  • Private photos served through addresses specific to each person, valid for 60 seconds, with access checked again.
    • {{- end}}
  • Separate moderation tool, protected by an account and a one-time code; actions are logged.
  • Technical logs with no IP address and no content.

Messages are not end-to-end encrypted: they are stored on the server.{{if on "photos_privees"}} Private photos are not end-to-end encrypted either.{{end}} No system is immune to every incident; in the event of a data breach, the authorities and the people concerned are informed as the law requires.

Links and codes received by email

  • To confirm your address or choose a new password, the email contains a button, a 6-digit code and, at the bottom, a long code for versions earlier than build 3. All three answer the same request: as soon as one has been used, the others no longer work.
  • The button opens a page on our server (api.proximas.be), with no script and no resource from another site. Opening the link confirms nothing: you have to confirm on the page. Technical logs record neither the link nor the code.
  • Button and long code: valid for 24 hours to confirm an address, 1 hour for a password. 6-digit code: 15 minutes; it stops working after 5 failed attempts, and an address is allowed 10 attempts per 24 hours.
  • The server keeps these links and codes only as hashes.

13. Minors

ProximaS is forbidden to anyone under 18. The server calculates age from the date of birth; during the beta, this date is declared with no external check. At sign-up, age is calculated at today's date in the phone's time zone, never more favourably than in Brussels time; the age shown to others follows today's date where the person looking is. An account suspected of belonging to a minor may be suspended, with no end date, pending an age check.{{if on "lot5_serveur_deploye"}} If it is deleted while the check is pending, a fingerprint of its email address prevents signing up with it again for 365 days.{{end}} If you think a minor is using the service, report it from the app or write to {{mail}}.
