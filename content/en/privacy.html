4. Location and distance

  • The app asks for an approximate location, only while you are using it. It does not follow your movements in the background.
  • The server rounds the coordinates it receives to a grid of about 500 m before saving them; the original coordinates are neither kept nor logged. Only the latest square is kept.
  • Other people never receive your coordinates, only a band: “less than 1 km” or “about N km”. In a conversation, this band is recalculated at most once a day.
  • With the “Hide my distance” option, no band is shown about you, either in the sky or on your profile, and no distance appears in your conversations, for either person. In the sky, your profile is not ranked by distance: it is offered, in a randomly chosen spot, to compatible people less than 25 km away, whatever their distance filter. So seeing you in their sky tells them you are within that radius. You still see the approximate distance of others in the sky and on profiles.
  • Invisible mode removes your profile from other people's sky and grid. Your conversations remain, and no distance appears in them, for either person. It also stops profile visits from being recorded.
  • Who can still open your profile. In invisible mode, or when your location has not been updated for more than 7 days, you appear neither in the sky nor in the grid. Your profile page and profile photos can then be viewed only by a person you have a conversation with, or a person you sent a signal that is still pending (neither accepted nor passed) and has not expired, with no indication of distance. A signal you send in invisible mode therefore opens your profile to the person who receives it. A signal you only received from someone does not give them this access. A block, in either direction, closes it.
  • Even an approximate distance can help a determined person guess your area. Hiding your distance removes this clue, but you can still appear in the sky of people less than 25 km away: by changing their location several times, someone may try to narrow down where you are. Invisible mode removes you from the sky and the grid, but does not close your profile to the people described above; block those you no longer want to show it to. None of these settings erases what others have already seen or noted, or guarantees that nobody can guess your area: your photos and messages can give clues too.
  • If you withdraw the “location” consent, the stored location is erased immediately and your profile is disabled.

6. What others see

  • Compatible people see your first name, your age, your approved photos, your answers to questions, your current moods and, unless you hide it, an approximate distance. The grid view of the sky shows your photo, first name and age. In invisible mode or with a location that is too old, access is more restricted and shows no distance (see who can still open your profile).
  • They see neither your email address, nor your date of birth, nor your location.
  • A person you write to receives your signals, messages and meeting suggestions. They can copy or screenshot them: do not send anything you would not want to see shared.
  • A blocked person no longer sees you and can no longer write to you. They are not told.

Profile visits: “Viewed your profile” and “Visit discreetly”

  • What counts as a visit: opening a person's profile, for example from the sky, the grid, a conversation or the list of your visitors. The two accounts and the date of the latest visit, to the nearest hour, are recorded; a new visit replaces the previous one.
  • What the visited person sees, in My profile, “Viewed your profile” (“A vu ton profil”): your first name, age, photo and the day of your visit, with no distance; they can open your profile. They receive no notification. The list shows 100 people at most, and only those who are still active, compatible, visible, with a recent location, and with no block between you.
  • “Visit discreetly” (“Visiter discrètement”, in My profile, off by default): your visits are not recorded, neither are the visits others pay you during that time, and you do not see who viewed your profile. Invisible mode has the same effect.
  • Visits already recorded are not erased by these settings. With discreet visiting, the visits you made earlier remain visible to the people concerned; invisible mode hides them while it is on. The visits you had received reappear when you leave these modes, if their retention period has not passed.
  • Retention: 30 days after the latest visit, or until one of the two accounts is erased. A block or a disabled profile does not erase them: they are no longer shown.
  • The list and the setting are in the up-to-date version of the app. With an older version, your visits are recorded in the same way; invisible mode prevents it.
{{- if on "photos_privees"}}

Private photos and albums

  • Who sees them: nobody, as long as you do not send them or share an album. Sending or sharing is only possible in a conversation open to both of you (accepted signal, active accounts, no block), to a person who has not turned on “Refuse photos”.
  • No prior check: nobody checks a private photo before it is sent or shared, and no automatic analysis of the image is performed.
  • Receiving: the photo appears blurred. This blur is a pattern created on the phone: nothing from the image is transmitted before the person touches it to display it.
  • Sending modes: normal (until you withdraw it), view once (10 seconds) or disappearing 24 hours after sending. A shared album follows your changes: an added photo becomes visible, a removed photo disappears.
  • Withdrawing: at any time you can withdraw a sent photo, delete a photo or an album, or remove access to an album. What has already been seen cannot be taken back. A block permanently closes sending and sharing between you; unblocking reopens nothing.
  • Refuse photos: this setting blocks new photos sent to you and hides albums already opened to you; photos you received directly before remain.
  • Screenshots: they are blocked on Android only, not on iPhone, and another device can always photograph the screen. Only send what you would accept seeing passed around.
  • Files and limits: images are re-encoded without metadata and stored separately. A photo sent from your phone's gallery does not go into your private gallery, but it is stored on the server. Sending and sharing (30 per 24 hours) and the space per account are limited.
  • After they disappear: a copy of a photo shown at least once is kept for a while (see retention periods).

11. Security

  • Encrypted connections (HTTPS) between your device and the service, through Cloudflare.
  • Passwords and tokens kept as hashes.
  • Location rounded before saving; photo metadata removed; photo addresses signed and temporary.
    • {{- if on "photos_privees"}}
  • Private photos served through addresses specific to each person, valid for 60 seconds, with access checked again.

Messages are not end-to-end encrypted: they are stored on the server.{{if on "photos_privees"}} Private photos are not end-to-end encrypted either.{{end}} No system is immune to every incident; in the event of a data breach, the authorities and the people concerned are informed as the law requires.
